Logos and brand identity that hold up everywhere

A logo is the smallest part of a brand. What matters is whether the whole thing still looks like you on a phone screen, a shop sign and a printed card. I design identities and the day-to-day visuals that come after them, and I have worked as head graphic designer for companies in several countries.

How it runs

Design gets decided in a few rounds, not endless ones.

Brief

What the business is, who it talks to, what it should feel like, and what it must not look like.

Direction

A small number of real directions, shown in context — on a phone, on a sign, on a post — rather than as floating logos.

Refinement

We pick one and sharpen it. Two rounds of changes are normal and included.

Handover

Vector and raster files in every size you need, plus a short guide on how to keep using them.

Questions

What do I actually receive at the end?

Vector files (SVG and PDF) and raster exports (PNG and JPG) at the sizes you need, the colour and type specifications, and a short usage guide. You own all of it.

Can you design to an existing brand?

Yes. A lot of the work is designing within a brand someone else set, whether that is a company style guide or the way a founder already posts.

Do you do print as well as digital?

Yes — cards, flyers, banners and backdrops, set up with bleed and print-ready colour rather than a screen export.

How many revisions are included?

Two rounds after a direction is chosen. Beyond that it is usually a sign the brief changed, and we rescope rather than keep redrawing.
